“How frequently does the wearied pilgrim put up the prayer, ‘O that I had wings like a dove, for then would I fly away and be at rest.’ But Christ does not pray like that; he leaves it to his Father, until, like shocks of corn fully ripe, we shall each of us be gathered into our Master's garner. Jesus does not plead for our immediate removal by death. He asks that we may do well in the world, but he never asks for us to be gathered in before we are ripe.”
*(Sermon on John 17:15)*

“The devil is not so concerned with the Christian’s fall into sin, for he knows the blood of Christ can cleanse and restore. What he truly delights in is the Christian’s fall into shame. When a believer is ensnared by shame, he turns his gaze inward, away from the cross, and rather than fleeing to God for mercy, he seeks to make amends by his own efforts, thinking he must do some good thing before he dares to return to the Lord. This is why those who wrestle with lust often regard it as the chief of sins, while the equally grievous sin of pride is overlooked.”

“No heaven will be so sweet as a heaven which has been preceded by torments and pains. Methinks the deeper draughts of woe we drink here below, the sweeter will those draughts of eternal glory which we shall receive from the golden bowl of bliss; the more we are battered … the more glorious will be our victory above.”
*(Sermon on John 17:15)*
